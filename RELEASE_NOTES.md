Athanor 0.10.1: what an independent review of Deep Time found, fixed.

Deep Time shipped last week. A review of its code, with simulations to settle the hard parts, confirmed the pieces most likely to be silently wrong — strand handling, the CpG context on each strand, every coordinate conversion, the genotype maths, the random allele draw — and found defects elsewhere that a thirty-fold benchmark could not see. They are fixed, each with the test that would have caught it.

- **The interval that decides genetic sex was not a 95% interval.** It ignored the counting noise in the X's own depth, which at low coverage is the whole uncertainty, and missed the truth about three times in four. It is now built properly, and the call is which copy number the data is nearer — one X or two — rather than a pair of fixed thresholds with a gap that a real female fell into.
- **Heterozygosity read too high at low and middling coverage**, by up to 44% at tenfold: sites too shallow for a homozygote were dropped from the denominator while heterozygous sites at the same depth stayed in the numerator. The depth floor is now where a homozygote can be called as confidently as a heterozygote, and the app says what it is.
- **Deamination measured against the reference read only the first chromosome** once it hit its read limit, then named them all. Each chromosome now gets its own share, and the background comes from bases far from both ends of their molecule.
- **Genotypes use each read's own base quality**, with a floor for what the sequencer cannot see: a damaged base is scored as a perfect read, and taking the qualities at face value turned two damaged reads in thirty into a heterozygote.
- Hard-clipped bases count towards a molecule's length; reads with no base qualities are not given one; the overlapping mates of a pair count once; the reference is checked against the alignment.
- Every damage rate now carries an interval and a minimum number of observations; every heterozygosity carries an interval and the number of sites behind it.
- Several cards said more than the code did: the sex card could quote an interval it had just called inconclusive, diploid mode reported no covered sites, and a card said contamination was measured when nothing measures it. They now say what is true, including that these calls carry reference bias and, when deamination is untreated, that it pushes the ancestry numbers one way.
- **The benchmarks now gate what can fail.** Agreeing with an all-sites call set proved little, since it is almost all homozygous reference: the archaic check now gates the sites that carry a non-reference allele (99.57% of 7,748, heterozygous sites 100% of 909), the share of the study's calls made here at all (94.6%), and transversion heterozygosity on hundreds of events rather than eighty (6.05 against the study's 6.03 per 100,000).
